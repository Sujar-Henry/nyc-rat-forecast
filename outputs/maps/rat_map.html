Where will NYC inspectors find rats?

On January 1, 2025, an XGBoost model looked back over 15 years of city rodent inspections and forecast every first-time inspection that followed. It knew nothing after that morning. One map shows where it expected inspectors to find rats; the other shows where they did.

Neighborhoods, ranked by expected risk

Share of first visits that found rats since January 2025, in neighborhoods with at least 200 of them. Expected is the model's forecast from January 1, 2025. Pick a row to find it on the map.

How this was made

Every row in the city's Rodent Inspection dataset is a visit to a property. This forecast covers first-time inspections and predicts one thing: will the result be "Failed for Rat Activity"? Inspectors fail a property for rat activity when they see active signs such as fresh droppings, burrows, runways, gnaw marks or live rats.

What it knew
Only what the city knew on January 1, 2025: each property's inspection and baiting history, recent results on the surrounding blocks, the neighborhood, and the time of year of each inspection. Nothing after that morning, including the results of the other inspections it was predicting.
How it learned
The same way it was tested: forecasts made every January 1 from 2010 to 2023, each checked against the two years that followed. A forecast made on January 1, 2024 decided when to stop training.
Why only first visits
Follow-up visits only happen after a property fails, so knowing one was coming would give the answer away.
Why it ran high
Inspectors found rats on 17% of first visits since January 2025, a clear drop from the years before. Working from those years, the model expected 23%. The maps compare each one against its own city-wide average, so they show whether it picked the right places.
What it isn't
A rat census. Inspections follow 311 complaints and the city's own sweeps, so this shows where inspectors find signs of rats, not where every rat lives.
Data: NYC Department of Health and Mental Hygiene, Rodent Inspection (NYC Open Data). Hexagons are H3 resolution 8, about 0.7 km² each; hexagons with fewer than 25 first visits since January 2025 are left blank.
